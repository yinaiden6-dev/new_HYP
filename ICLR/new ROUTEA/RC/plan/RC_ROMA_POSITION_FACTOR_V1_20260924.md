## Fixed interventions (12 arms)

1. NATIVE and ZERO anchors.
2. AMP_FLAT: keep each patch's P direction, replace its norm with a fixed per-side RMS radius derived from all common41 existing unlabelled P summaries (one constant for each side).
3. AMP_PERMUTE: keep P directions, permute the patch norms within each side using seed20260924+side; preserve the norm histogram and total concentration.
4. GLOBAL X/Y plus/minus: translate all encoded target positions by 0.125 normalized units on one axis. Apply the corresponding orthogonal sin/cos phase rotation; preserve every patch norm and every frequency's power.
5. LOCAL X/Y plus/minus: use a fixed balanced sign field to translate different source patches in opposite directions. The paired plus/minus arms have exactly the same average elementwise squared perturbation dose as the two global signs. This contrast tests relative arrangement beyond a coherent coordinate translation.

Primary descriptive contrasts: LOCAL minus GLOBAL averaged across the predetermined axes and signs, separately for direct logM target margins and frozen-head M_ONLY/FULL_UV target margins. Report every arm, all eight outcome-independent queries, group resampling uncertainty only as exploratory. Case audit reports all three original rescues separately. Native remains baseline, not the best observed arm.

Important limits: translation moves encoded coordinates relative to unchanged content, J and the head's learned coordinate conventions. Local phase changes preserve within-patch concentration but break cross-patch consistency. Even a loss under GLOBAL may reflect coordinate-frame sensitivity. Norm equalization and direction changes may produce off-manifold head inputs. Do not call P norm an entropy, P direction a pure coordinate, or declare RoMa/geometry universally indispensable. Other spatial information in A/J remains intact.
